This paper designed, instantiated and evaluated an explainable decision support framework for multi-item FMCG inventory planning. Its decision model sets order quantities and safety stocks jointly under ordering, holding, expected-shortage and quantity-discounted purchasing costs, a procurement budget, warehouse capacities and service targets, and it is convex within each price regime, which yields a lower bound that certifies the quality of any recommended policy. An ABC algorithm adapted to the problem returned feasible policies in every run and the lowest-cost policies on most synthetic instance groups, while generic penalty-based GA, PSO and ABC rarely returned feasible policies under binding resources; the ablation traces the gain to inventory-specific constraint handling and a log-scale perturbation rather than to the bee-colony search as such. Replaying the policies on held-out demand showed that model feasibility does not guarantee realised service, mainly because of the continuous-review assumption, which the decision artifact reports next to cost drivers, binding resources, the certified gap and rule-based recommendations. The framework runs in a working decision support system in which planners retain and record the final decision. The evidence is synthetic and technical; application to real distributor data and a user study of the explanations are the next steps.
